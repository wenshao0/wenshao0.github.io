Silicon Labs Jumps 49% After Texas Instruments Acquisition

Silicon Laboratories Inc. (SLAB) jumped 49% to a four-year high on Feb. 4 after Texas Instruments Inc. agreed to buy the chip designer for $7.5 billion in cash as it looks to expand its wireless connectivity business.

The deal brings Silicon Labs’ wireless chip business into Texas Instruments, one of the world’s largest analog chipmakers, expanding its reach in Internet-of-Things markets including electric vehicles, smart-home systems and medical devices. The acquisition adds roughly 1,200 wireless products to Texas Instruments’ lineup, according to company disclosures.

For Texas Instruments, the acquisition marks its biggest transaction since its $6.5 billion purchase of National Semiconductor in 2011. While the deal expands Texas Instruments’ presence in wireless chips, Bloomberg Intelligence analysts say the company’s long-term growth remains tied to a sustained recovery in industrial and automotive markets, which together account for roughly 66% of revenue.

Haviv Ilan, chief executive officer of Texas Instruments, said the deal is expected to generate more than $450 million in annual cost savings within three years of closing. “They have done the work to build the stack of chips, software and firmware at the application level to support hundreds of end equipment,” Ilan said, adding that it could take Texas Instruments decades to build a comparable ecosystem.
