Credo Has Steepest Drop Since Jan. 2025 on Nvidia Optical Shift

Credo Technology Group (CRDO) fell about 15% to close at $97.30 on March 3, the steepest one-day drop since January 2025, as investors worried that Nvidia Corp.’s push toward optical networking could threaten demand for the company’s copper-based interconnect products.

The San Jose, California-based company reported record quarterly revenue of $407 million, up 201% from a year earlier, driven by demand for AI infrastructure from customers such as Amazon.com Inc. and Microsoft Corp.

Credo makes chips that move data between graphics processing units inside AI servers in data centers, allowing hundreds or thousands of GPUs to work together on the same computing tasks. Most of its products rely on copper cables that transmit electrical signals between chips. Nvidia is pushing optical networking, which uses light signals to transmit data faster and over longer distances.

To strengthen its position, Credo acquired Austin-based CoMira Solutions to gain control over semiconductor IP used in high-speed, lower-power connections between chips in AI data centers. Chief Executive Officer Bill Brennan said the deal will help develop more energy-efficient connections between chips.
