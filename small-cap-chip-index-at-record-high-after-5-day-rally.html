Small-cap chip index at record high after 5-day rally

The Russell 2000 Semiconductors Index, which includes small- and mid-cap U.S. chip companies, reached an all-time high after five consecutive days of record closes.

The Federal Reserve’s March meeting notes, released April 8, reinforced expectations for interest rate cuts in the second half of 2026, a positive for smaller chip companies, which tend to be more sensitive to borrowing costs. The Russell 2000 Semiconductors Index reached 2776.9 on April 17.

Another key driver is AI demand, which is creating a widening gap within the semiconductor industry, boosting companies tied to data centers while leaving those focused on smartphones or automotive markets behind. FormFactor, a maker of chip-testing equipment, is valued at 165 times its earnings and has risen 146% this year, while Skyworks, which supplies mobile-phone chips, trades at 13 times earnings and is down about 7% so far this year.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. Chief Executive Officer C.C. Wei said on Thursday that demand for AI chips is “extremely robust.” He added that AI is shifting from conversational responses to performing multi-step tasks, which demands more power from the chips at every stage.
